Reading with less noise

By Sam Writer

Attention is a limited resource, and the modern web competes for every scrap of it. Sidebars, autoplaying videos and pop-ups pull the eye away from the sentence you were trying to finish.

Reading tools try to give some of that attention back. The simplest ones strip the page down to its text, set comfortable type, and stay out of the way.

Others go further. Bionic text bolds the first letters of each word, which some readers find helps their eyes move along the line without slipping.

A spotlight dims everything except the paragraph you are on. It is a small change, but it turns a wall of text into a single, finishable step.

Auto-pacing moves a gentle highlight through the words at a steady rate. You can speed it up, slow it down, or pause it whenever a thought needs room.

None of these tricks work for everyone. The point is choice: a reader should be able to shape the page to fit how their own mind works today.

That is the idea behind this small project. It is built in the open, it asks for as few permissions as possible, and it never sends your reading anywhere.

If it helps you finish one more article than you otherwise would have, it has done its job.

What comes next

Future versions may add focus sessions, a site blocker and a reading list that remembers where you stopped.
